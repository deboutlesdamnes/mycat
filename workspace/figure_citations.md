# Pattern Analysis

## 1. Where these questions come from
The `Adapted from … ©Year Publisher/Society` attribution format is the signature of
**AAMC official MCAT practice material** (Section Bank / Practice Exams). AAMC builds
passages by adapting text, figures, and tables from **real published research** rather
than writing them from scratch.

## 2. The "corpus" is the primary peer-reviewed literature
There is no single database being pulled from. Instead, the figures/tables trace back to
~24 individually selected primary sources, heavily concentrated in high-impact society
journals:

| Discipline | Recurring sources |
|---|---|
| Biochemistry / cell biology | *J. Biological Chemistry* (×2–3), *Biochemistry* (ACS), *J. Clinical Investigation*, *Cell*, *Cell Host & Microbe*, *Science* |
| Physics | *American Journal of Physics* (AAPT) — the canonical pre-med physics modeling papers |
| Clinical / epidemiology | *British Heart Journal*, *Medical Care*, *Seminars in Nuclear Medicine* |
| Psychology / social science | *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, *J. Child Psychology & Psychiatry*, *Comparative Cognition & Behavior Reviews*, APA journals, *Medical Care* |
| CARS | Books/essays (*Dyson*, *Tuchman*, *Rogler*) rather than journal articles |

## 3. Publisher / society clusters (recurring copyright holders)
- **Elsevier** — *Cell*, *Cell Host & Microbe*, *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, *Seminars in Nuclear Medicine* (most common single publisher)
- **American Society for Biochemistry and Molecular Biology** — *JBC*
- **American Association for the Advancement of Science** — *Science* (×2)
- **American Chemical Society** — *Biochemistry*
- **American Psychological Association** — social-psych sources (×2)
- **American Association of Physics Teachers** — *Am. J. Physics* (×2)
- Plus SAGE, Guilford Press, Pergamon Press, ASCI, AAP, AMA, W.B. Saunders

## 4. Temporal pattern
- **Physics**: older canonical papers (1973, 1996).
- **Bio/Biochem**: tight **2007–2012** cluster of recent mechanistic papers (Hong 2007,
  Ohashi 2012, Wei 2011, Zhang 2011, Mukhopadhyay 2012), plus two landmark classics
  (Nathans 1986 *Science*; Sod-Moriah 1983).
- **Psych/Soc**: classic/landmark studies 1972–2011 (Lieber & Baron 1972 through
  Schmader 2010).

## 5. Bottom line
The figures are adapted from **primary research papers in high-impact biomedical,
physics-pedagogy, and behavioral-science journals** (PubMed-indexed literature), with
CARS passages drawn from books and essays. The consistent seed corpus is:
**JBC / Science / Cell / Cell Host & Microbe / JCI / Biochemistry** for the molecular
sciences, **American Journal of Physics** for physics, and **Elsevier / APA / SAGE**
behavioral-science journals for psychology/sociology.

# Impact-Factor Tiers & Textbook Canon

## Do the non-CARS sections cluster around high-impact journals?

Yes — but the strength of the cluster differs by section.

**Bio/Biochem (Section 3): the clearest "elite journal" cluster.** Five of its eight
citations are top-tier:
- *Science* (Nathans 1986; Mukhopadhyay & Linstedt 2012) — elite multidisciplinary, IF ~50+
- *Cell* (2012) — elite, IF ~60+
- *Cell Host & Microbe* (Zhang 2011; Kim) — high specialty, IF ~30
- *Journal of Clinical Investigation* (Wei 2011) — high specialty, IF ~15
- *JBC* (Hong 2007) and *Biochemistry* (2008) — mid-tier IF (~3–5) but canonical for enzymology/protein structure.

**Psych/Soc (Section 4): high-impact behavioral journals.** Most citations are top-tier
for their field: *Behaviour Research and Therapy* (IF ~5–6), *Current Directions in
Psychological Science* (IF ~7–9), *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry* (IF ~7–8),
APA journals (*JPSP*/*Developmental Psychology*, IF ~5–8), plus mid-tier *Medical Care*
(IF ~3) and the niche *Comparative Cognition & Behavior Reviews*.

**Chem/Phys (Section 1): a mix of pedagogy classics and mid-tier clinical journals.**
- *American Journal of Physics* (Bunton 1996; Hobbie 1973) has a low IF (~1–2) **by design** — it is a physics-education journal chosen for pedagogical value, not research impact.
- *Seminars in Nuclear Medicine* (IF ~3–4), *British Heart Journal* (IF ~5–7), *JBC* (IF ~5), *Biochemistry* (IF ~3) are respectable but not elite.

So the impact gradient is: **Bio/Biochem > Psych/Soc > Chem/Phys**, with CARS (books/essays) outside the journal-impact scale entirely.

## Are these findings classics in textbook canon?

There is a clear two-track selection:

**Track 1 — textbook-canon classics (older landmark studies).**
- Nathans et al. 1986 *Science* — molecular genetics of color vision; in every genetics/neuroscience text.
- Hobbie 1973 / Bunton 1996 *Am. J. Phys.* — the axon equivalent-circuit (RC cable) model; the core of every MCAT physics/neurobiology prep.
- Lieber & Baron 1972 — televised violence and children's aggression; classic social psychology (Bobo-doll tradition).
- Williams, Whiten, Suddendorf & Perrett 2001 — mirror neurons, imitation, and autism; canonical in developmental neuroscience.
- Hudson & Rapee 2001 / Kendall & Suveg 2006 — childhood anxiety and its cognitive-behavioral treatment (Rapee, Kendall are field-defining).
- Schmader 2010 — stereotype threat; the standard modern review of a textbook phenomenon.
- Jenny 2008 / Giardino 2011 — built on Kempe's 1962 "battered-child syndrome," a pediatric canon.
- Epstein et al. 2003 *Medical Care* — landmark health-disparities study; canonical in health-services research.
- Banerjee et al. 2001 — Tc-99m radiopharmaceuticals; canonical in nuclear medicine.
- Sod-Moriah et al. 1983 — pineal gland and thermoregulation; an older chronobiology classic.

**Track 2 — recent, high-impact primary research (influential but not yet "general textbook" canon).**
Mostly the **2007–2012 Bio/Biochem cluster**: Hong 2007 (pantothenate kinase structures),
Wei 2011 (CDKAL1/tRNAᴸʸˢ → diabetes), Zhang 2011 (perinuclear actin and malaria var gene
expression), Ohashi 2012 (AMF-26 Golgi inhibitor), Mukhopadhyay & Linstedt 2012
(manganese/Shiga toxin). These are cited in specialized reviews but have not entered
general textbooks.

**Conclusion.** AAMC's selection is a deliberate blend: **canonical, textbook-grade
landmark studies** (especially in physics, psychology, and classic genetics) plus
**fresh, high-impact mechanistic papers** (especially in biochemistry/cell biology,
2007–2012) — all drawn from the elite tier of their respective fields.
